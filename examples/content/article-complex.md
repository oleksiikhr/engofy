# Negotiating My First Job Offer Abroad

1. Research the company's *average* salary range before you sit down at the table.
2. Rehearse your pitch out loud — it lands differently than it reads on paper.
3. Don't accept the first number; **always** push back at least once.

- Bring up your **current** salary only if the recruiter asks first.
- Stay calm even if the offer feels *disappointingly* low.

Three years ago, I flew from Toronto to Berlin to interview for a data-engineering role at a fintech startup called Solvex. I'd read dozens of articles about negotiation, but nothing prepared me for how nervous I felt walking into the lobby. The recruiter, Mateusz Kowalski, greeted me warmly and walked me through the building before we sat down. Most recruiters won't offer more unless you actually ask, but the offer on the table was already ten percent above my old salary. I remembered my notes anyway and calmly turned the offer down, explaining that the market rate for someone with my experience was closer to seventy thousand euros. He didn't flinch; he simply picked up the phone and called his manager.

Two days later, Mateusz called back with a revised offer that was **_significantly_** better than the first. I still couldn't quite believe it, so I asked my old colleague Priya to look the contract over before I signed anything. She spotted a clause about relocation costs that I'd completely missed, and her advice ended up saving me almost two thousand euros. Looking back, I think the turning point was refusing to jump at the first offer — once I'd broken the ice by asking a real question, the whole conversation became a genuine back-and-forth instead of a one-sided pitch.

## What I'd do differently

I started the job in March 2020, exactly one month before the company announced it was going fully remote. Looking back on it now, I'm convinced that a single afternoon of preparation was worth more than the previous three years of my career combined.
